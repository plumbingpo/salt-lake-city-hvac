From the hallway, a furnace that will not heat looks the same no matter what broke. Inside the cabinet, though, everything happens in a strict order: inducer on, pressure switch closed, igniter glowing, gas valve open, burners lit, flame proven, and finally the blower sending heat through the house. Most of the diagnosis comes down to finding the broken link. In Salt Lake City homes, it tends to be the same handful of culprits: a flame sensor coated in oxide, a hot surface igniter cracked by thermal cycling, a pressure switch held open by a tired inducer or a plugged condensate trap, or a control board with a burned relay.

Rather than guess, Salt Lake City HVAC tests the sequence stage by stage. We read igniter resistance, measure flame signal in microamps, check that the pressure switch responds to real inducer draft, confirm voltage at the gas valve, and test the blower and its capacitor. Because this is a fuel-burning appliance, the safety basics come with every visit: the heat exchanger gets inspected, gas connections get leak-tested, and draft gets verified. You cannot tell a furnace problem from a furnace hazard by looking at the thermostat. Before we leave, temperature rise is measured against the data plate so airflow and flame are matched as the manufacturer intended.

Salt Lake City HVAC services natural gas, propane, and electric furnaces from all major brands, from standard 80 percent units to condensing high-efficiency models with their traps, drain lines, and dual pressure switches. Our trucks carry the parts that actually fail, such as igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors, so most homes have heat again before we leave. When to Call for Furnace Repair in Salt Lake City

The sooner you notice these red flags, the easier and cheaper the problem is to resolve. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • The blower runs constantly but the air never warms
  • Yellow, wavering burner flames where they should be sharp blue
  • Grinding or high-pitched squeals from the blower
  • Water, rust stains, or black soot around the flue or cabinet
  • Repeated shutdowns just seconds after the burners light
  • The furnace clicks and glows but the burners never light
  • A bang or boom when the burners ignite late
  • Short cycling every few minutes without reaching the setpoint

The Benefits of Professional Furnace Repair

A furnace combines gas, flame, high voltage, and a flue in one cabinet, and the failure that matters most is the one you cannot see from outside: a compromised heat exchanger passing combustion gases into the air your blower pushes through the house. Delayed ignition that booms at light-off stresses that exchanger further every morning it goes unfixed. Meanwhile the diagnostic traps stack up: a furnace that trips its high limit may have a bad switch, or it may be protecting itself from a clogged filter or a dying blower, and replacing the switch in the second case just removes a safety while leaving the fault.

Our experienced technicians treat every symptom as a question about the whole system. Flame signal gets measured rather than assumed, so a healthy sensor is never replaced while a cracked igniter or weak gas valve skates by. Draft is verified at the pressure switch and the flue. Temperature rise is checked against the data plate, which is how airflow problems get caught before they shorten the exchanger's life. And the gas side is leak-tested with instruments, not a sniff and a shrug. The result is a furnace that has been proven safe and correct under a full cycle, and an honest report if the unit is nearing the point where replacement beats another repair.

Maintenance Tips for Furnace Repair

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Keep storage, lint, and especially chlorine-based products away from the furnace closet
  • Give the furnace room to breathe, since a crowded utility space starves the burners of air
  • Test your CO detectors monthly and make sure each floor has one
  • Notice how the furnace sounds at startup, because a quiet light-off is healthy and a boom is not
  • Keep the condensate trap and drain clear on high-efficiency units to prevent lockouts
  • Have the furnace checked every autumn, while a weak igniter or fouled sensor is still an easy fix
  • Put in a fresh filter monthly through the heating season, the cheapest protection your furnace can get
